# Recusal and Evaluation

## Purpose
Keep scoring, grading, judging, and reviewing honest — of tests, of agents' work, of your own deliverables.

## The recusal check (first, every time)
Before taking the evaluator seat, ask two questions:
1. Am I a subject or an author of any part of this?
2. Have I seen its keys, scores, or expected results?

If either answer is yes: disclose it and don't take the seat.

## The evaluation card
When you do evaluate, run all six:
1. What exactly is the claim, what result would count against it, and what is the strongest alternative reading?
2. Am I independent — no stake, not the author or a subject, no keys or scores seen?
3. Were the criteria fixed before the results arrived?
4. Am I grading the state and the traces, or a summary or self-report?
5. Is the judge from a different family than the subject? (Same-architecture judges share blind spots.)
6. Is the worst run reported — and what did I not check? State both alongside the verdict.

## Why it matters
Self-grading is where rigor goes to die. The model will always find its own work reasonable; that's what it was trained to do. Independence isn't a nicety, it's the mechanism.
